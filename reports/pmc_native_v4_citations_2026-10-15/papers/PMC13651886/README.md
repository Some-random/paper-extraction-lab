**RESULTS / Wnt signaling is down-regulated in OTULIN-deficient KCs**

> OTULIN was initially identified as a modulator of canonical Wnt signaling regulating embryonic angiogenesis ( [bib_ref]The linear ubiquitin-specific deubiquitinase gumby regulates angiogenesis — Rivkin et al.[/bib_ref] ). To determine whether OTULIN and linear (de)ubiquitination modulate Wnt responses in adult tissues, we analyzed Wnt signaling in the skin of mice with OTULIN-deficient KCs (∆^(Ker)OTULIN mice). We previously demonstrated that ∆^(Ker)OTULIN mice develop inflamed skin lesions that progress into well-differentiated squamous carcinomas ( [bib_ref]OTULIN maintains skin homeostasis by controlling keratinocyte death and stem cell identity — Hoste et al.[/bib_ref] , [bib_ref]OTULIN inhibits RIPK1-mediated keratinocyte necroptosis to prevent skin inflammation in mice — Schunke et al.[/bib_ref] ). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of our published single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset comparing total skin of wild-type (WT; Cre-negative OTULIN^(FL/FL)) and ∆^(Ker)OTULIN mice ( [bib_ref]OTULIN maintains skin homeostasis by controlling keratinocyte death and stem cell identity — Hoste et al.[/bib_ref] ) revealed Wnt signaling as one of the top down-regulated GO terms in OTULIN-deficient KCs (Fig. 1A and fig. S1A). When probing expression levels of specific Wnt mediators in our scRNA-seq dataset, we indeed observed marked down-regulation in OTULIN-deficient KCs relative to control KCs (Fig. 1B). Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) analysis of epidermal tail lysates confirmed the down-regulation of Wnt target genes in ∆^(Ker)OTULIN epidermis (Fig. 1C and Table 1). In agreement, expression levels of several members of the TCF/LEF family, key downstream mediators of canonical Wnt signaling, were also significantly reduced in ∆^(Ker)OTULIN epidermis (Fig. 1D). Immunoblotting for nonphosphorylated β-catenin, indicative of stabilized β-catenin and active Wnt signaling, confirmed reduced Wnt activation in epidermal tail lysates from ∆^(Ker)OTULIN relative to control mice (Fig. 1E). Wnt activation in KCs from adult skin is most prominent during the growth phase of HFs, also referred to as anagen, culminating in nuclear expression of LEF1 ( [bib_ref]Wnt signaling in skin development, homeostasis, and disease — Lim et al.[/bib_ref] , [bib_ref]De novo hair follicle morphogenesis and hair tumors in mice expressing a truncated beta-catenin in skin — Gat et al.[/bib_ref] ). Immunofluorescence staining of anagen skin indicated nuclear LEF1 in WT HFs, whereas no nuclear LEF1 was observed in anagen-like HFs in lesional ∆^(Ker)OTULIN skin (Fig. 1F and Table 2). When probing HF cell populations (outer and inner layer, bulge and upper HF) of our scRNA-seq data for Wnt target gene expression, the down-regulation of Wnt target gene expression in OTULIN-deficient KCs was even more pronounced than in the total KC population (Fig. 1G). In addition to OTULIN, SHARPIN has also been implicated in stabilizing β-catenin ( [bib_ref]SHARPIN stabilizes β-catenin through a linear ubiquitination-independent manner to support gastric tumorigenesis — Zhang et al.[/bib_ref] ). Therefore, we quantified expression levels of Wnt mediators and target genes in SHARPIN^(cpdm/cpdm) epidermis and control epidermis. Down-regulation of canonical Wnt signaling was observed in SHARPIN^(cpdm/cpdm) epidermis, supporting a role for linear ubiquitination in promoting Wnt signaling in adult KCs (fig. S1B). β-catenin also performs Wnt-independent roles at the plasma membrane ( [bib_ref]Mechanical force-driven adherens junction remodeling and epithelial dynamics — Pinheiro et al.[/bib_ref] ). We assessed the levels of cytosolic β-catenin in OTULIN-deficient and control epidermis and observed higher levels in control epidermis, indicating that OTULIN promotes canonical Wnt signaling in the skin (fig. S1C). Together, these data demonstrate that OTULIN promotes canonical Wnt signaling in adult KCs.

**RESULTS / OTULIN regulates the stability of the survival factors TCF3 and TCF4**

> To investigate how linear ubiquitination of β-catenin influences KC survival, we focused on two Wnt target genes, Tcf3 and Tcf4, previously shown to protect cells from death by cooperative functioning with β-catenin to drive transcription ( [bib_ref]Wnt/β-catenin signaling, disease, and emerging therapeutic modalities — Nusse et al.[/bib_ref] , [bib_ref]Tcf3 and Tcf4 are essential for long-term homeostasis of skin epithelia — Nguyen et al.[/bib_ref] , [bib_ref]Armadillo coactivates transcription driven by the product of the Drosophila segment polarity gene dTCF — van de Wetering et al.[/bib_ref] ). Tcf3 and Tcf4 were significantly suppressed in OTULIN-deficient mouse KCs (Fig. 1D). Previous studies demonstrated that genetic ablation of Tcf3/4 in KCs results in a marked increase of dying cells in neonatal mouse epidermis ( [bib_ref]Tcf3 and Tcf4 are essential for long-term homeostasis of skin epithelia — Nguyen et al.[/bib_ref] ). Immunoblotting for TCF3 revealed a reduction in protein expression in OTULIN-deficient primary mouse KCs relative to WT KCs (Fig. 6A). Proteasome inhibition stabilized TCF3 in both WT and OTULIN-deficient cells, indicating that TCF3 stability is regulated by K48-linked ubiquitination in KCs. Both Wnt-repressive and Wnt-activating properties have been attributed to TCF3/4 ( [bib_ref]Tcf3 and Tcf4 are essential for long-term homeostasis of skin epithelia — Nguyen et al.[/bib_ref] ). TCF3 expression in primary KCs strongly correlated with the levels of activated β-catenin levels, suggesting that TCF3 functions as a Wnt activator under these conditions (Fig. 6A). Consistently, Tcf3 and Tcf4 levels were reduced in epidermal lysates obtained from ∆^(Ker)OTULIN skin relative to control epidermis, and no significant difference in Tcf3 or Tcf4 levels was observed between control epidermis and epidermis from ∆^(Ker)OTULIN ΔNβcat mice (Fig. 6B). These findings indicate that the protection from cell death observed in KCs from ∆^(Ker)OTULIN ΔNβcat mice relative to ∆^(Ker)OTULIN mice could be linked to altered TCF3/4 expression levels. To test this hypothesis, OTULIN KD HaCaTs were reconstituted with TCF3 by lentiviral transduction (fig. S4A). Reconstitution of TCF3 significantly reduced TNF cytotoxicity in OTULIN KD cells; however, cell death in these reconstituted KCs remained higher than in control KCs (Fig. 6C). This indicates that TCF3/4 contribute to the cellular response to TNF, with evidence suggesting a modulatory, rather than exclusively protective, role in cell viability. Immunofluorescence staining for TCF4 in tail whole mounts of control mice confirmed TCF4 expression in adult normal HFs ( [bib_ref]Tcf3 governs stem cell features and represses cell fate determination in skin — Nguyen et al.[/bib_ref] ), whereas no TCF4 could be observed in ∆^(Ker)OTULIN skin (Fig. 6D). In contrast, TCF4 expression in the tail epidermis of ∆^(Ker)OTULIN ΔNβcat mice was comparable to that observed in control mice. Notably, co-staining for TCF4 with cleaved caspase-3 revealed that TCF4-expressing KCs were not undergoing apoptosis, as no double-stained cells were observed (Fig. 6D).
