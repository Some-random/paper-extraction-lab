**Results / Replication of known genetic associations**

> The significant pQTLs discovered in our study were compared to three previous proteomics GWAS. These include two large-scale affinity-based proteomics GWAS using Olink Explore 3072 PEA in the UK Biobank [bib_ref]Plasma proteomic associations with genetics and health in the UK Biobank — Sun et al.[/bib_ref] and SomaScan v4 in the deCODE study [bib_ref]Large-scale plasma proteomics comparisons through genetics and disease associations — Eldjarn et al.[/bib_ref] , and a smaller-scale mass-spectrometry proteomics GWAS using the MS-based Seer Proteograph platform in the Tarkin study [bib_ref]A genome-wide association study of mass spectrometry proteomics using a nanoparticle enrichment platform — Suhre et al.[/bib_ref] . For all significant pQTLs in the current study, we performed lookup analyses in these studies, where the same protein was assessed and also demonstrated significant associations. We replicated 46 of 199 (23.12%), 64 of 275 (23.27%) and 10 of 25 (40%) pQTLs with the Sun et al. [bib_ref]Plasma proteomic associations with genetics and health in the UK Biobank — Sun et al.[/bib_ref] , Eldjarn et al. [bib_ref]Large-scale plasma proteomics comparisons through genetics and disease associations — Eldjarn et al.[/bib_ref] , and Suhre et al. [bib_ref]A genome-wide association study of mass spectrometry proteomics using a nanoparticle enrichment platform — Suhre et al.[/bib_ref] , studies, respectively (see Table 1). Effect sizes of the overlapping associations correlated highly between platforms (R_(Pearson) = 0.94 (MS:UKB [bib_ref]Plasma proteomic associations with genetics and health in the UK Biobank — Sun et al.[/bib_ref] )); 0.90 (MS:deCODE cohort [bib_ref]Large-scale plasma proteomics comparisons through genetics and disease associations — Eldjarn et al.[/bib_ref] ); 0.89 (MS:Tarkin [bib_ref]A genome-wide association study of mass spectrometry proteomics using a nanoparticle enrichment platform — Suhre et al.[/bib_ref] ) (Fig. 2). A list of the overlapping proteins-SNP associations can be found in Supplementary Data 3, 4 and 5.

### Article paragraphs explicitly referring to this table

**Methods / Proteomics**

> 439 proteins and protein groups were considered; 133 (30.3%) of these were uniquely mapped to one UniProt ID (termed “proteins”), with the remaining 306 (69.7%) mapping to multiple potential UniProt IDs (termed “protein groups”). 199 of these protein groups represent different isoforms of the same protein, and 107 mixed protein groups where the signal mapped to different proteins. 138 out of the 199 isoform signals were mapped to proteins that were identified more than once (repeated isoform groups). We use a “GX” naming system, where “G” signifies a group and “X” refers to a number to identify these repeated isoform groups (Table 2). For example, Apolipoprotein D was identified twice, with one of the signals annotating to three isoforms [UniProt identifiers: P05090, C9JF17, C9JX71] and was given the name Apolipoprotein D (G1) and another signal mapped to two isoforms [UniProt identifiers: P05090.C9JF17], therefore was named Apolipoprotein D (G2). 61 signals were annotated to protein isoform groups that did not repeat; these therefore only have a “G” after their protein name. We used a “PG X” naming system to identify the 107 signals that map to multiple different proteins. For example, one signal is annotated to multiple immunoglobulins (Immunoglobulin lambda variable 3-27, Immunoglobulin lambda variable 3-25 and Immunoglobulin lambda variable 3-16), and therefore is referred to as protein group 1 (PG 1). As can be observed with the PG1 example, in many cases, the protein groups with mixed labels contain related proteins. In some instances, a protein is identified as a unique signal and as several protein groups. For example, one mass spectrometry signal mapped to Alpha-1-antitrypsin identified via a single UniProt ID P01009; therefore, it was named Alpha-1-antitrypsin. However, five other mass spectrometry signals mapped to five different combinations of UniProt IDs that were all related to Alpha-1-antitrypsin and were therefore named Alpha-1-antitrypsin G1 to G5. Given that these groups originate from different signals, they were maintained as separate entities in downstream analysis. Annotation of the 439 proteins considered in this study is provided in Supplementary Data 11.
