# Biological continued pretraining: data sources and token budgets

How biological foundation models turn research papers, web content and existing datasets into training data. The main comparison focuses on **continued pretraining (CPT)**: starting with a pretrained model and training it further on domain data. Data-curation studies and released models appear together because both inform this choice.

**These are reported source budgets, not uniformly biology-only token counts.** Some include clinical literature, general text, other sciences or non-text modalities. The modality descriptions and linked source notes identify that scope. A paper’s full training total cannot be treated as a biology-only amount when that subset is not separately reported.

The authors/institutions column gives compact credits; the study pages retain the fuller lists. Dataset names appear below their token count; a “+N” link opens the longer source list. **Overall paper assessment** considers the contribution and originality, technical soundness, evaluation, reproducibility, and clarity of the whole paper in its publication context. **Strong:** substantial contribution, convincingly executed. **Solid:** worthwhile work with meaningful limits. **Mixed:** substantial strengths alongside weaknesses that constrain the overall case. **Weak:** fundamental problems dominate. These are provisional editorial judgments, not official reviewer scores; institutional prestige and relevance to this project do not determine them.

### Reading the counts

- **Online papers:** text acquired directly from publishers or publication repositories, including abstracts, XML, PDF and LaTeX. The names below each amount identify the source; “via … (reused)” means the text belongs in the existing-dataset column instead.
- **Web URLs:** non-paper webpages acquired directly, including raw web archives.
- **Existing datasets:** reused corpora and biological databases. Paper text imported through S2ORC or The Pile belongs here; it is not counted twice. These columns describe **how the study obtained the data**, not just what the text discusses.
- **0** means the described data has no separate acquisition through that route. **NR** means the token amount is not reported or cannot be established. Neither a known source name nor a paper count supplies a missing token count.
- **Dataset size** counts the prepared corpus before training repeats. **Tokens used in training** counts consumption, including repeated passes: a 10B-token dataset read three times gives 30B training tokens, not 30B of distinct data. The former measures data volume; the latter measures how much training read it. For BioMedLM these figures are 34.6B and 300B.
- **M / B** mean million / billion; **≈** means approximate; **> / ≥** indicate a lower bound. Tokenizers differ. Sequence/image tokens and planned token positions are labeled explicitly. A total may include material without a disclosed category split.

**Other modalities** describes data already included in the acquisition columns; it is not an additional source category. Protein/DNA/RNA sequences, molecular strings, structure tokens and figure images are distinguished on each study page, with counts where reported. A “protein data” budget can include annotations and structures as well as sequence tokens. Later SFT/encoder inputs are explicitly distinguished from the counted CPT stage.

**Why a category can be smaller than the total:** BioMatrix’s existing-dataset lower bound is **176.7B = 25.6B general text + 77.4B protein data + 73.7B molecule data**. The other **127.7B** comprises 79.7B scientific text and 48.0B cross-entity/interleaved data; their exact paper/web/dataset split is not disclosed. These are identified but unallocated components, not missing training tokens. See [the source breakdown](papers/biomatrix.md#token-budget-breakdown). Elsewhere, “≥” likewise means a lower bound, and a dataset name without a token amount remains NR. BioMatrix’s 25.6B general-text slice is FineWeb-Edu; BioMamba’s existing-dataset component is general replay. NatureLM and SciReasoner also cover sciences beyond biology. None of those whole-row totals is a verified biology-only budget.

Gravity-bio reports separate literature and biological-database components but not the full training mixture. LOGOS reports a corpus spanning pretraining and post-training; its CPT-only amount is not disclosed. BioMamba’s figure counts packed positions, including padding. Biomed-Enriched’s 33.6B is the training budget **per experimental variant**. PMC-InterCPT contributes a paper/figure curation method evaluated on medical and scientific tasks; it is not evidence that clinical filtering is appropriate for all biology.
